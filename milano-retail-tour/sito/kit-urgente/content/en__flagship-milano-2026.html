Milan’s 2026 flagships: what to borrow, on a small scale, from the big brands

30 November 2026 · MiRT Radar · On the ground in Milan

In 2026 Milan saw flagships open or reopen that do more than sell: they let you touch the materials, organise runs, customise jeans in front of the customer. You do not need 200 square metres and an architect to copy the idea. For each of the five cases below there is a specialist-store version you can try on a small budget.

Why look at flagships

According to ICSC and McKinsey, more than 40% of Gen Z and millennial shoppers say in-store experience makes them more likely to buy from a retailer, while 37% of consumers put convenience first. A store has to choose what to offer: speed or discovery. Flagships choose discovery, and they make it visible. That is why they are a good laboratory to watch.

1. L’Ottocento, viale Piave: let people touch

The bespoke kitchen maker from Padua opened in April at viale Piave 4: 200 square metres on two levels with a material library, where visitors handle wood species, raw blocks and furniture sections, plus a meeting room for architects and clients.

On a small scale: a sample corner where customers touch and compare, with a card for each material. It works for furniture, fabrics, eyewear, footwear, bicycles.

2. Hoka, Brera: create a fixed date

The running shoe brand’s first Italian flagship opened in April in Brera, and since 2 May it has hosted a weekly run club that prepares runners for city races, starting with the Stramilano.

On a small scale: a fixed appointment, always on the same day: a run, a class, a tasting, a workshop. Customers no longer come in only when they need something.

3. Liu Jo Circle, corso Vittorio Emanuele: customise in front of the customer

Reopened on 24 September with a new concept, the flagship has a corner where jeans are customised with crystals, embroidery and laser finishes, guided by a “Denim Creator” who starts with a consultation and ends with a one-off piece.

On a small scale: a made-to-measure service with a dedicated person and a recognisable name: engraving, hemming, frame fitting, bike setup. It is what online cannot do.

4. Pop Mart, via Dante: give people a reason to come back

The collectible-character brand, led by Labubu, opened its second Milan store in via Dante in September, after corso Buenos Aires, and is preparing a “large and immersive” store near San Babila. Its stated aim: turning stores into experiences built around its characters.

On a small scale: a calendar of new arrivals announced in advance, with one release or limited edition a month. Anticipation brings customers into the store.

5. Ralph Lauren, via della Spiga: become a destination

This autumn the brand opens its first home-only store in via della Spiga, next to its flagship, Ralph’s bar and childrenswear boutique: “not just a point of sale, but an experiential destination”.

On a small scale: widen your offer by one step, towards a nearby category your customers already look for. The bookshop with a café, the bike shop with a workshop, the wine shop with tasting evenings.

And in 2027 Alo, the Californian yoga apparel brand, arrives in piazza Duomo: more on that later.

Where AI comes in

Not in the installations, but behind the scenes: managing bookings for the fixed appointment and customer reminders, preparing material cards, drafting the release calendar. What flagships do with a whole team, a specialist store can do with one person and an assistant.

What to do on Monday

  1. Pick just one of the five ideas, the one closest to what you already do.
  2. Set a date before the end of January and tell your best customers, in person or on WhatsApp.
  3. After a month, count who came back. If it works, make it permanent.
