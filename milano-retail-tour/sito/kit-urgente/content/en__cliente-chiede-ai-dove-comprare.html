Your customer asks AI where to buy: does your store exist?

26 October 2026 · MiRT Radar · AI at the counter

In Italy, product discovery through traditional search fell by 15% in less than a year, while discovery through new channels such as AI assistants grew by 38%. For a specialist store this means one thing: if the information about your store online is vague or out of date, AI will recommend someone else. Here is what is changing, and six moves to get found, almost all of them free.

What is changing

Salesforce measured how Italians discovered products between August 2025 and May 2026. Traditional search fell by 15% and brand websites by 7%. New channels, meaning AI assistants, AI inside social media and delivery apps, grew by 38%.

This is not just a young people’s thing. According to an Adyen survey of 2,000 consumers in May 2026, 34% of Italians used an AI assistant to shop in the past year. Among Gen Z the figure is 52%.

It happens in store too. 79% of Italian shoppers use their smartphone while shopping, and 12% ask an AI assistant for advice right in the aisle. Meanwhile Google has brought its AI Mode to Italy, which answers many questions with a written reply instead of ten links.

Why it matters for specialist stores

The question is changing. People used to search for “optician Milan”. Now they ask: “where can I find an optician near Porta Venezia who does progressive lenses and is open on Saturdays?”. The assistant builds its answer from the Google profile, the website, reviews and articles.

And it names two or three stores, not ten. Coming eleventh, in practice, means not existing.

Here the specialist store has an advantage and a problem. The advantage is expertise, which is exactly what makes an answer useful. The problem is that this expertise is often not written down anywhere: it lives in the heads of the owner and the staff. Chains have tidy profiles and catalogues; independents have more to say, but say less of it online.

An example

Illustrative example. A niche perfumery in the Isola district has a Google profile with the generic category “cosmetics store”, its August opening hours and no list of brands. Someone asks an assistant: “where can I buy niche perfumes near Isola?”. The answer will mention the stores that wrote “niche perfumery” and the names of their brands on their website or profile. Our perfumery, which may well carry more of them, does not appear.

Where this is heading

In the United States, Google’s AI Mode can already phone local stores to ask whether a product is in stock. For now the feature is US-only, but it shows the direction: the assistant does not just read, it checks.

For the 2026 holidays Adobe expects traffic from AI tools to US retail websites to grow by 130% year on year. The numbers in Italy are smaller, but the curve is the same.

What to do on Monday: six moves

  1. Fix your Google profile. A precise primary category (“optician”, “niche perfumery”, “hardware store”) plus secondary ones. Correct opening hours, including December. Recent photos. A description that lists services and brands.
  2. Write down your expertise. On your website, a page with services, brands, specialities, neighbourhood and the questions customers really ask you, in their own words.
  3. Look after your reviews. Ask happy customers for them and reply to all of them within a few days. Name the service in your replies: “thank you for choosing our frame adjustment”.
  4. Make everything consistent. The same name, address, phone number and opening hours on your website, Google, Instagram, Facebook and online directories. Different hours on different channels cost you trust, with people and with assistants.
  5. Show what you stock. Even without an online shop you can put your main products online. With Merchant Center, Google lets you show products available in your store.
  6. Run the test. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google’s AI Mode: “where can I buy [your product] near [your neighbourhood]?”. Note who gets mentioned and why, and repeat it every month.

And where does AI come in?

None of the six moves needs artificial intelligence: they are store basics, written down properly. That is our method: retail first, then AI.

AI comes next, and it saves time: it helps write descriptions, drafts replies to reviews (which a person then checks) and keeps an eye, every month, on what assistants say about your store and your competitors.
