Reviews, the AI Act, accessibility: the three 2026 rules that affect every store

23 November 2026 · MiRT Radar · The specialist store

In 2026 three rules changed the daily work of anyone who sells in Italy: the new law on online reviews, the AI Act as amended by the Digital Omnibus, and accessibility of digital services, now supervised by AgID. None of them needs a legal department. All of them need you to know what you do today with reviews, with AI and with your website.

1. Reviews: they count if they arrive within 30 days

Italy’s annual SME law, no. 34/2026, in force since 7 April, devotes six articles to online reviews. A review is lawful if it is written by someone who actually used the service, within thirty days, and with no discount or incentive in return. If it comes with a receipt or invoice it is presumed authentic, and proof only has to be shown after a report.

Businesses can report unlawful reviews to the platform, and trade associations can become “trusted flaggers”, with a priority channel. The case that made the most noise: on 23 March the Italian competition authority AGCM fined Trustpilot 4 million euros for an unfair commercial practice (the company has announced an appeal).

Note: the law was written mainly with restaurants, hotels and tourist attractions in mind, and AGCM’s guidelines, which will clarify requirements and penalties, have not been issued yet. Whether and how it applies to your store is a question for your adviser or trade association. One thing already applies to everyone: no discounts in exchange for reviews.

2. The AI Act: training stays, chatbots must say they are AI

The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in force since 27 July, amended the AI Act. For a store, two things change.

  • Training (Article 4). Companies using AI no longer have to “ensure” a sufficient level of competence: they must take measures to support it among their staff. There is no minimum level to certify, but the obligation remains, and the Commission and Member States must help SMEs in particular.
  • Transparency (Article 50). From 2 August 2026, anyone talking to a chatbot must know they are talking to an AI. If you put an assistant on your website or on WhatsApp, customers must understand that straight away.

The heaviest rules, on high-risk systems, have been postponed to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028, and they cover cases such as recruitment or credit, not your shop window or catalogue.

3. Accessibility: everyone must be able to use your website and online shop

Since 28 June 2025 the European Accessibility Act, transposed in Italy by Legislative Decree 82/2022, also covers private digital services, e-commerce included. In May 2026 AgID published its supervision regulation: a case starts from a complaint or an inspection, and for some violations fines reach 40,000 euros.

Microenterprises providing services are exempt: fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet of no more than 2 million euros. But if you grow, or sell to businesses that are covered, the exemption does not protect you. And a website everyone can use sells more anyway.

What to do on Monday

  1. Remove from your messages, signs and newsletters any discount linked to a review, and ask for the review within a few days of the purchase.
  2. List the AI tools you and your staff use, and organise an hour of basic training: who uses them, for what, and what is off limits.
  3. If you have a chatbot, check that its first sentence says it is an AI assistant. Then try your website with the keyboard only: if you cannot buy, there is work to do.

This article is not legal advice: for specific cases, talk to a professional.
