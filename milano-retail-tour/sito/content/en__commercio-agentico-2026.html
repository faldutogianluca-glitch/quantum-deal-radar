AI that shops for your customers: what really happened in 2026, and what to do

16 November 2026 · MiRT Radar · Retail radar

43% of Italians would let an AI handle an entire purchase, payment included, and 80% of Italian retailers say they are ready. But 2026 also showed the limits: OpenAI scaled back checkout inside ChatGPT, while Visa and Google pressed ahead. For a specialist store the right move is not to chase a platform: it is to get its catalogue, prices and availability in order, because that is what agents read.

What “agentic commerce” means

An AI agent is an assistant that does not just recommend: it acts. The customer writes “reorder the same shampoo” or “find me trail shoes under 150 euros, size 42, available by Saturday”, and the agent searches, compares and, if authorised, pays.

Italians are readier than you might think

According to an Adyen survey of 2,000 consumers and 500 Italian retailers in May 2026, 43% of Italians would let an AI handle an entire purchase, payment included. It is not just Gen Z: among Baby Boomers the figure is 41%.

Those who are not ready fear losing control (48%), see no need (39%) or worry about mistakes (36%). On the retail side, 80% of Italian retailers say they are open to it and 48% plan to invest within a year. Their main concern, for 37%, is losing the direct relationship with the customer.

What really happened in 2026

  • OpenAI slowed down. In March it scaled back checkout inside ChatGPT, launched in October 2025 with Etsy and Walmart: “Instant Checkout is moving to Apps”, a spokesperson said. Buying inside a general-purpose chat turned out to be harder than expected.
  • Payments moved ahead. Visa completed the first payments made by AI agents in Europe with more than 30 issuers, including Barclays, HSBC UK and Revolut, and merchants such as lastminute.com. Each payment is tied to a verified cardholder and their explicit authorisation.
  • Standards are multiplying. At NRF in January Google presented its own commerce protocol, alongside the one backed by Stripe and OpenAI. Almost three in four attendees at one session said they had projects under way or planned.
  • Retailers are building their own assistants. Home Depot has Magic Apron on its website, Ralph Lauren has Ask Ralph. URBN started with a few categories, dresses and denim, rather than the whole catalogue.

What it means for a specialist store

An agent buys from the stores it can read: product, price, availability, delivery or pick-up times. If that information is not online, or differs from one channel to another, the store simply is not part of the choice.

The weak spot is well known: according to Salesforce, 44% of Italian companies do not sync inventory in real time and 37% have inconsistent prices and promotions across channels.

Illustrative example. A running store stocks 40 shoe models but shows 12 online, with prices from six months ago. A customer asks their assistant for a model the store has in the window: the assistant does not know, and buys elsewhere.

What to do on Monday

  1. Pick one category, your best seller, and put every product online with code, price and up-to-date availability.
  2. Check that prices and promotions are the same at the till, on the website and on social media.
  3. Set a rule for the customer: if an agent buys, who looks after them afterwards? You protect the relationship with service, not by staying out of new channels.
