Second-hand and trade-in: recommerce comes to the specialist store

7 December 2026 · MiRT Radar · Retail radar

In 2025, 65% of Italians bought or sold at least one second-hand item, in a market worth 27.2 billion euros. In the United States, second-hand clothing is growing four times faster than new. For a specialist store, second-hand is not necessarily a competitor: with trade-in it can become the reason a customer comes back, and buys new.

The numbers in Italy

According to the Second Hand Economy Observatory by Ipsos Doxa for Subito, published in May 2026, 28.2 million Italians bought or sold second-hand in 2025. The market is worth 27.2 billion euros, 1.2% of GDP, and 54% of it goes through online channels.

The main reason is saving money, for 62% of buyers: on average they spend 44% less than for new, up to 50% in categories such as clothing, vintage, bicycles, books and children’s products. Among those who buy second-hand online, 52% buy in the sports and hobby category.

What is happening in the United States

According to ThredUp’s 2026 Resale Report, in 2025 second-hand clothing grew about four times faster than the clothing market as a whole, and it will reach 78.8 billion dollars by 2030. Gen Z and millennials will drive more than 70% of that growth. And 48% of second-hand shoppers already use AI tools while they shop.

What the big players have been doing for years

  • IKEA takes back its own used furniture in exchange for an in-store voucher, with a value that depends on the item’s condition, and resells it in its bargain corner.
  • Decathlon runs Trocathlon in Italy: people bring their used sports gear to the store, a technician values it and the store puts it back on sale.

In both cases the point is not to make money on second-hand: it is to bring the customer back to the store, with a voucher in their pocket or a reason to walk in.

Why specialist stores start ahead

To take back used goods you need to know how to value them, and expertise is exactly what a specialist store already has. Bikes, skis, musical instruments, camera gear, pushchairs, books: people who sell them new know what a used piece is worth.

Illustrative example. A bike shop takes back a customer’s old bike and gives them a voucher for a new one. The old bike goes to the workshop, is serviced and goes back on sale with the shop’s guarantee. The customer upgrades sooner, and the old bike finds a new owner, perhaps a younger one.

Note: second-hand goods sold by a store in Italy also carry the legal guarantee. For used goods it can be shortened, but not below one year: check the details with your adviser.

Where AI comes in

A first estimate from the photos the customer sends, a product card for each used item, the listing to publish on your website or a marketplace. AI drafts it, the final valuation stays with the technician, in front of the item.

What to do on Monday

  1. Pick one category where you are good at valuing used goods.
  2. Set a simple rule: trade-ins are paid as an in-store voucher, with three value bands depending on condition.
  3. Try it for a month with one shelf of guaranteed second-hand items, and count how many vouchers come back to the till.
