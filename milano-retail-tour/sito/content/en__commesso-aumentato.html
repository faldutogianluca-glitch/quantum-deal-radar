The augmented sales assistant: what Ace Hardware and Walmart teach a specialist store

9 November 2026 · MiRT Radar · AI at the counter

87% of retail revenue still runs through the physical store, and at Christmas 77% of Italians choose the store. That is why big retailers are putting AI in the hands of their sales staff, not in their place: Ace Hardware has given an assistant to more than 2,300 stores, and Walmart is training all 2.1 million of its employees on AI. Specialist stores already have the most valuable thing, expertise: AI helps make it available to everyone, every day.

The store still matters, and the sales assistant even more

According to IHL Group, 87% of retail revenue is still tied to the physical store. And for 2026, empowering store associates is retailers’ number three technology priority: 45% list it among their top investments.

In Italy the picture is the same. For Christmas shopping 77% of consumers prefer the store, but they walk in with a phone in hand: 79% use it while shopping and 12% ask an AI assistant for advice right in the aisle (Salesforce). Customers arrive informed. The sales assistant needs to know at least as much as their phone.

What the big players are doing

Ace Hardware, the American hardware chain, has rolled out “Hey ARMA”, an AI assistant for staff, to more than 2,300 stores: the associate asks, the assistant answers on products, procedures and availability.

Walmart has announced it will train all 2.1 million of its employees on AI tools. Its customer assistant, Sparky, more than doubled its weekly users in one quarter, and people who use it spend about 35% more per order, according to CEO John Furner.

The point is not to copy Walmart. It is to notice where they invest: not in robots that replace people, but in people who answer better and faster.

Why specialist stores start ahead

In a chain, AI has to make up for the limited experience of staff who change often. In a specialist store the experience is there: in the owner and the long-serving staff. The problem is that it does not multiply. On a Saturday afternoon there is one owner and ten customers.

Illustrative example. In a neighbourhood hardware store, the assistant who joined in September does not know which wall plug is needed to hang a cabinet on plasterboard. Today he asks the owner, who is busy with another customer. With an assistant that knows the catalogue and the owner’s answers, he answers on his own in a few seconds, and the answer is right.

Three practical uses, simplest first

  1. The owner’s answers, always available. An internal assistant that knows product sheets, price list and the answers to frequent questions, written by people who have worked in the store for years.
  2. The customer card. What they bought, which size they wear, when their check-up is due: the assistant sees it before saying hello, with the customer’s consent.
  3. The message after the purchase. AI drafts the thank-you note or reminder; the assistant reads it, personalises it and sends it.

In all three cases a person decides. That is the MiRT AI rule: every AI output goes through someone who checks it.

What to do on Monday

  1. For one week, ask your staff to note the customer questions they could not answer on their own.
  2. With the owner, write the right answer to the first twenty: it is the basis of any future assistant, and it already helps train new staff today.
  3. Check that every member of staff has had basic AI training: the AI Act has required it of companies that use AI since February 2025.
